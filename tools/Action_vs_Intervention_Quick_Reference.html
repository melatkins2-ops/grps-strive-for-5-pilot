Quick reference · Attendance decision-making

Action vs. Intervention

A completed task is not necessarily an intervention. The distinction is whether the response is designed to address the reason a scholar is missing school.

Action

Something staff did—such as making a call, sending a letter, sharing a resource, holding a conference, or making a referral.

Example: Called the caregiver about unreliable transportation.

Remember: An action is not, by itself, an intervention.

Intervention

An intentional response designed to remove or reduce the identified barrier, change a condition or behavior, build a needed skill or relationship, or address the documented reason for absence.

Example: Developed a primary and backup transportation plan, assigned follow-through, and monitored whether arrivals improved.

What should the attendance plan document?

  • Identified root cause and evidence, including scholar/family voice
  • Matched intervention and responsible owner
  • Dosage or frequency and implementation fidelity
  • Evidence that the barrier is reducing
  • Attendance response and the decision at 10-, 20-, and 30-day checkpoints for active Tier 2/3 plans
