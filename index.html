Root causes of absence

Barrier

Something prevents the scholar from getting to school.

Aversion

Something about the school experience causes avoidance.

Disengagement

The scholar lacks connection, relevance, or motivation.

Misconception

The scholar or family misunderstands attendance expectations or impact.

Action vs. intervention

Action: something staff did—such as a call, letter, email, or referral.

Intervention: something intentionally designed to remove a barrier, change a condition, strengthen a relationship, build a skill, or address the reason for absence.

Content validation & system alignment

Validated operating rules:

  • Strive for Less Than 5 is universal prevention messaging—not the MTSS intervention threshold.
  • Synergy is the official operational attendance source; contextual information and scholar/family voice help explain why.
  • Targeted plans name a primary root cause: Barrier, Aversion, Disengagement, or Misconception.
  • Root cause determines the intervention and owner; attendance pattern/severity informs intensity.
  • Active targeted plans review implementation fidelity, barrier reduction, and attendance response at 10/20/30 school days.
  • Low fidelity → repair implementation. Good fidelity + barrier unchanged → adjust. Barrier reduced + attendance improving → continue/fade. New/severe need → intensify/coordinate.
  • Legal/truancy escalation is not the organizing framework for attendance intervention.

ACTION → INTERVENTION PRACTICE

Common documentation mistakes

Action: Sent an attendance letter.
Intervention: After identifying a misconception, created an attendance goal/habit plan with progress feedback and review.
Action: Called the caregiver about transportation.
Intervention: Created a primary and backup transportation plan, completed a warm handoff, and monitored arrival.
Action: Referred the scholar to FSS.
Intervention: FSS identified the family-facing barrier, implemented the matched plan, coordinated resources, and monitored barrier reduction.
Action: Shared a counseling resource.
Intervention: Implemented a predictable re-entry/check-in plan while completing the behavioral-health connection and monitoring avoidance.
Action: Held an attendance conference.
Intervention: Used the conference findings to create a specific barrier-removal plan with an owner, dosage, goal, and review date.
Action: Gave an incentive.
Intervention: The barrier-matched support remains the intervention; any incentive is only a documented component when appropriate—not the intervention itself.

COMMUNITY RESOURCE CONNECTIONS

Connect the barrier to the right support

Use community partners to help carry out a matched attendance intervention. A referral or resource link is an action; the intervention is the intentional plan that removes or reduces the identified attendance barrier and is monitored for impact.

ACTION

Something staff did

Examples: sent a letter, made a phone call, shared a resource, or made a referral.

Important: an action is not, by itself, an intervention.
INTERVENTION

A response designed to change the attendance condition

It removes or reduces a barrier, changes a behavior or condition, or directly addresses the situation/root cause contributing to absence.

Monitor whether attendance and the identified barrier actually improve.

WARM HANDOFF CHECK

Before calling it an intervention, answer five questions

  1. Barrier: What specific attendance barrier or condition are we addressing?
  2. Intervention: What will actually change or reduce that barrier?
  3. Partner: Who is helping deliver the intervention?
  4. Owner: Who at GRPS is responsible for follow-through?
  5. Review: What will we check at 10, 20, and 30 days to know whether it worked?
